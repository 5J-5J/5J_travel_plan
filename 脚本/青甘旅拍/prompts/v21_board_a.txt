Create a cinematic shooting-reference contact sheet for an original 90-second solo travel film in northwest China, Qinghai and Gansu. A precise 3-column by 2-row grid, six equal photographic panels, each panel 16:9; overall canvas extremely wide 8:3 (ideally 2048x768). Thin black dividers, no text, no labels, no watermarks. Realistic film stills with tactile detail, restrained color, muted blue mountains, pale earth, ochre-olive jacket, black trousers; same anonymous adult traveller throughout, mainly back or partial side view, no celebrity likeness. Every image is an achievable camera composition, not a surreal morph or fantasy location. The purpose is to demonstrate how separate shots acquire meaning through juxtaposition.
Top row is a visual rhyme between three openings. Panel 1 extreme close view of an open parked-car door/window frame: two large dark diagonal edges form a narrow bright V-shaped wedge of daylight, a hand only at the lower edge. Panel 2 distant bare mountains whose two shoulders form an analogous bright V-shaped gap in the sky, same position and angle. Panel 3 close rear three-quarter view of the traveller's loose jacket collar, two fabric edges make a V opening; real mountains sit behind the shoulder, face out of frame. These are three different physical scales, not digitally transformed objects.

Panel 4 a hand holds a small unprinted off-white paper folded once into a modest tent/ridge, low oblique side light clearly shows the fold, its main ridge ascending from bottom left to upper right. Panel 5 a natural low sand ridge with the same diagonal and light direction. Panel 6 medium rear-side view of traveller lowering that paper out of the lower frame, an extensive mountain horizon now visible over their shoulder; the paper is a modest real prop, no writing, no giant paper mountains. All three remain calm and tactile. Do not make the whole canvas 16:9; the six individual panels are 16:9.
